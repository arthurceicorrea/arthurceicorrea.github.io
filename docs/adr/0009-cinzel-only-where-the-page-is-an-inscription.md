# 9. Cinzel only where the page is an inscription

- Status: accepted
- Date: 2026-11-25; amended 2026-11-27 (stage 14: the article page)

## Context

The page uses two typefaces: Cinzel, drawn from Roman inscriptions and capitals only, and EB Garamond, a book face. Until stage 8 of [`docs/PLAN.md`](../PLAN.md), Cinzel set almost every short text: the motto, the name, the section titles, and also the menu, the language switcher, the buttons, "See details", "Copy" and the skip link, all as tracked-out capitals.

Two problems followed:

- **The motto had no rank.** *Sapientis est ordinare* is the most characteristic thing on the page, but it was set barely larger than the section titles and the menu, in the same capitals, and the name right below it repeated those capitals at a similar size, so nothing on the page stood out from the rest.
- **The interface read as generated.** Small tracked-out capitals on every label, and words joined by middle dots (`PT · EN · ES · IT`, `DEVOPS · IA APLICADA · SEGURANÇA`), are among the commonest marks of machine-made pages, whatever their subject.

## Decision

**Cinzel is kept for what is an inscription:** the motto, its attribution and the section titles. Everything the visitor uses (menu, language switcher, buttons, details toggle, copy button, skip link) is set in EB Garamond, in sentence case; the language codes in small capitals.

**The name is set in EB Garamond, in sentence case and a little larger,** as the author under an epigraph on a title page. Two other options were compared and set aside: Cinzel in sentence case, whose lowercase letters are small capitals, so the name stays an inscription; and EB Garamond in spaced capitals, which keeps the look of a sign. The name's own entrance animation, which closed the spacing between its capitals, is removed: lowercase letters are not spaced out, and animating the spacing changed the width of the name on every frame, which could rewrap it mid-animation. The name now fades in like the rest of the introduction, leaving the gold rule under the motto as the page's one moment of motion.

**The motto is the one loud element.** It is set large, in two fixed lines, *SAPIENTIS·EST / ORDINARE*, with a Roman interpunct between the words of the first line, as in Latin inscriptions. The dot is generated by CSS with empty alternative text, so screen readers still read the three words. The middle dots leave the language switcher and the line of crafts, which becomes a short italic phrase with commas.

**On an article page** (amended at stage 14 of [`docs/PLAN.md`](../PLAN.md)), two more inscriptions: the word *Article* above the title, set like a section title; and a **drop cap**, the first letter of the text as a three-line initial in gold Cinzel. An initial carved at the head of a text is an inscription letter, and Cinzel is drawn from such capitals; it is one letter per article, so the motto keeps its rank on the home page. The title and the headings of an article stay in EB Garamond, as the name does. The drop cap is drawn by CSS (`::first-letter`), so the word stays whole for screen readers and search.

Colours, contrasts and texts do not change. The 404 page, whose code and title are themselves inscriptions, does not change either. Nor do the CVs ([ADR 8](0008-cvs-printed-by-the-build-in-a-legible-typeface.md)): they have no motto to compete with, the name is the only title on the page, and Cinzel there keeps the CV visibly related to the site.

## Consequences

- The first thing a visitor sees is the motto, and the rest of the page is quieter around it.
- Interface labels are easier to read: lowercase words in a book face, at body size, instead of small spaced capitals.
- The interpunct belongs to the motto only; adding middle dots elsewhere would undo the decision. On an article page the date and the reading time are separated by a semicolon, not a dot.
- The motto takes more height on the first screen; on a 390 px phone the name still appears without scrolling.
- The name differs between the site (EB Garamond) and the CVs (Cinzel); the section titles keep the two related.
- Changing the motto's words would need the template's spans and the two-line rule revisited.
